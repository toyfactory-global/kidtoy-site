**Short answer:** every buyer compares range and magazine capacity. The spec that determines whether you make money is **seal quality** — because a leaking water gun is not a slow seller, it is a return.

## What buyers compare

Three numbers show up in every quotation:

1. **Range** (metres)
2. **Magazine / tank capacity**
3. **Battery life** (shots or minutes)

They are useful, but they are measured by suppliers under favourable conditions — flat angle, no wind, full charge. Treat a quoted range as a *relative* number for comparing two guns from the same supplier, not as an absolute promise to your customer.

## What actually determines returns

**Seals and joints.** Water under pressure finds every gap. The failure modes are consistent:

- Water seeps into the battery compartment and kills the electronics
- The magazine coupling drips at the joint, soaking the user's hand
- The pump housing cracks at the screw posts after a few drops

None of these show up in a spec sheet. All of them show up in your returns rate.

**How to test before a container order:** fill, fire continuously for several minutes, then leave it full and lying on its side for an hour. A gun that survives a full magazine left on its side is a different product from one that was only tested upright.

## Magazine format — the real product decision

| Format | What it is | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **Drum magazine** | Removable drum, clicks on underneath | Fast reload, strong visual, extra part to lose |
| **Fixed tank** | Tank built into the body | Fewer parts, fewer failures, slower refill |
| **Bottle feed** | Screw-on bottle | Cheapest, most leak-prone at the thread |

The drum format is currently the strongest shelf signal — it photographs well and reads as "high capacity". It is also the format with the most assembly tolerance, so the coupling quality is the thing to check.

## Battery and waterproofing

An electric blaster with a battery in a wet environment needs a sealed compartment, not just a cover. Ask specifically:

- Is the battery compartment gasketed, or only covered?
- Does the cover have a screw fixing or a push clip? (Push clips fail first.)
- Is the cell replaceable, and what type?
- Is charging done with the battery in or out?

A USB-rechargeable unit removes the cost of disposable batteries but puts the charging port on the wet side of the product — check where the port sits.

## Weight and grip, by age

A gun that is too heavy for a six-year-old gets held with two hands, aimed downward and abandoned. Weight distribution matters more than total weight: a balanced 700g pistol outperforms a nose-heavy 500g one.

If your range spans ages 3–8 and 8–12, you need two form factors, not one. A "pro" format with recoil and a large drum is a different product from a first blaster, even though both are electric water guns.

## What to ask before ordering

1. Where is the battery compartment, and is it gasketed?
2. What is the drop-test standard the factory applies?
3. Is the drum coupling a screw fit or a push fit?
4. What is the carton quantity and CBM? (Water guns are awkward shapes — density is poor.)
5. What is the dead-on-arrival policy per carton?

Question 2 is the one nobody asks. A factory that drops-tests will answer with a height and a surface. A factory that does not will answer with an adjective.
