**Short answer:** bubble machines are low-density products with a battery and often a bottle of liquid inside. That combination means you pay on **volumetric weight**, you may need lithium documentation for air, and you can turn a routine shipment into a damaged-goods claim if the liquid leaks.

## Problem one: the box is bigger than the product

Air freight and express charge whichever is greater — actual weight or volumetric weight:

```
volumetric kg = (L × W × H in cm) / 6000
```

Take a real carton from our catalogue — the dinosaur egg bubble machine, 72 pcs per carton:

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Carton size | 84.5 × 43 × 70 cm |
| Actual gross weight | 29.4 kg |
| Volumetric weight | (84.5 × 43 × 70) / 6000 = **42.4 kg** |

You are charged on **42.4 kg**, not 29.4 kg. That is a 44% surcharge on every carton, purely because the product is bulky for its weight.

**Consequence:** bubble machines, plush and large-format plastic should go by sea. Air freight is for samples and for urgent top-ups, and even then you should expect to pay on volume.

## Problem two: lithium batteries

A USB-rechargeable machine contains a lithium-ion cell. Toy cells in this category are small — our sunflower machine uses a 3.7V 500mAh cell, about **1.85 Wh**. That is well under the 100 Wh threshold that separates the simple shipping routes from full dangerous-goods handling.

In practice this usually means the shipment can move under the "batteries contained in equipment" provisions at Section II level, which many forwarders handle routinely. But:

- **It is the forwarder's call, not the factory's.** Ask your forwarder before you book, not after.
- **Air is stricter than sea.** Sea freight for a contained small cell is generally straightforward; air requires the documentation to be correct.
- **Detached batteries are treated differently from installed ones.** If the factory packs batteries loose in the carton, the classification changes.

One practical option: order the version **without a battery** (AA-powered) for air shipments and the rechargeable version for sea. Confirm both variants exist before you commit to a packaging design.

## Problem three: bubble liquid

Machines that ship with solution included are a better retail product and a worse shipment:

| Approach | Advantage | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-filled 100ml bottle in the box | Complete gift, no extra purchase | Weight, leakage risk, possible liquid restrictions |
| Concentrate sachet | Lighter, lower leak risk | Customer must add water |
| No liquid | Cleanest shipment | Product is incomplete at retail |

Leakage is the expensive one. A burst bottle inside a carton does not damage one unit — it damages the cartons stacked under it, and you find out at the destination, not at the factory. If you ship liquid, ask explicitly what the bottle seal is and whether the bottle is bagged inside the box.

## Sea versus air, decided properly

| Situation | Method | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Full container of machines | Sea (FCL) | Volumetric weight makes air uneconomic |
| Mixed pallet for a test order | Sea (LCL) | Only if you can wait the transit time |
| Samples before a PO | Express | Small quantity, accept the volumetric penalty |
| Urgent replenishment mid-season | Air | Budget on volumetric kg, not actual kg |
| Rechargeable units only | Sea | Avoids lithium air documentation entirely |

## A note on carton efficiency

Two suppliers can quote the same unit price and deliver very different landed costs because of how the product is packed. A machine that folds down, or a gun that ships with the drum detached, can cut CBM per unit substantially.

When you compare quotes, put the numbers side by side:

```
cost per unit shipped = ex-factory price + (freight per CBM × CBM per unit) + duty
```

A supplier 10% cheaper on unit price but 25% worse on CBM is the more expensive supplier.

## What to put in your inquiry

Ask for these five, and you can price the freight yourself before you talk to a forwarder:

1. Carton dimensions, L × W × H in cm
2. Units per carton and gross weight per carton
3. Total carton count and CBM for your order quantity
4. Battery type, capacity in Wh, and whether it is installed or loose
5. Whether bubble solution is included, and in what container
