**Short answer:** most first-container problems are decided before the deposit, not during production. This is the list to work through in that window — and if any item has no answer, it is a question to send rather than an assumption to make.

## Specification

1. **Item numbers** confirmed against the catalogue, not against a description.
2. **Age grade** stated, with the basis (small-parts assessment, intended use).
3. **Colour and material** named, not "as photo".
4. **Sample approved in writing**, with the date recorded.
5. **Label text** quoted verbatim, including warnings, in the destination language.

## Carton and freight

6. **Pieces per carton**, from the export record.
7. **Carton dimensions**, measured rather than estimated.
8. **Total CBM** for your exact quantity.
9. **Gross weight per carton** — and whether volume or weight will bind.
10. **Container fill** calculated; decide whether a mixed load is needed.

## Compliance

11. **Which standard applies** to this product in this market — toy or cosmetic.
12. **What documentation exists today** for this specific item.
13. **What would need testing**, and the lead time.
14. **Country of origin marking** and importer details on the artwork.

## Commercial

15. **Trade term** named (FOB / CIF / DDP) and what it includes.
16. **Deposit percentage and balance trigger**, in writing.
17. **Bank account confirmed** through a second channel, name matching the invoice.

## Execution

18. **Ready date** agreed as "ready for shipment", not arrival.
19. **Inspection booked** against that date, with the AQL and the inspector named.
20. **Documents list** agreed: invoice, packing list, bill of lading, conformity documents.

## The dates that get forgotten

Three, every time:

- **The inspection booking.** It has to be scheduled against the production finish, and inspectors book up.
- **The vessel booking.** A missed weekly sailing is a seven-day delay with no upside.
- **The free-time clock** on the container at destination, which starts whether or not your warehouse is ready.

None of these is technical. All of them cost money when they are discovered late.

## The one question that covers most of it

If you only ask one thing before paying a deposit, ask this:

> Please confirm, for these item numbers: pieces per carton, carton dimensions, total CBM for the quantity, production window from deposit, the HS heading you ship under, and what conformity documents exist for these items today.

A supplier who answers all six in one reply has the paperwork under control. A supplier who answers three and skips three is telling you what the order will feel like — and it is cheaper to learn that now.

## What to do with the answers

Put them in one document, dated, and treat it as the reference for the order. When something differs later, the conversation is about a specific line rather than about impressions, which is the difference between a ten-minute resolution and a lost season.
