**Short answer:** a toy importer works about four to five months ahead of the shelf. That means the calendar is not organised by season but by ship date, and the Chinese New Year shutdown sits in the middle of the year as a hard wall.

## The pattern

| Order placed | Goods ship | On shelf | What it is for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jun–Aug | Sep–Oct | Nov–Dec | Q4 gifting, Christmas |
| Sep–Nov | Dec–Jan | Feb–Mar | Spring, Easter |
| Nov–Dec | Feb–Mar | Apr–Jun | Summer water play |
| Feb–Apr | May–Jun | Jul–Sep | Southern-hemisphere summer, back-to-school |
| Any, small | Any | Any | Replenishment on proven sellers |

Read the columns as linked, not as separate decisions. The only date you control completely is the order date.

## The Chinese New Year wall

Production stops for one to three weeks and returns gradually. Freight space tightens on both sides. Anything that has to be on a shelf in March or April must be finished and booked before the holiday — which means the order was placed in the preceding autumn, at the same moment every other buyer is competing for the same slots.

If a season starts in Q1 and the order is not placed by November, the plan is already air freight.

## Q4 is decided in summer

The Christmas order is the largest of the year for most gift ranges, and it is placed in the northern summer. Buyers who start in September are buying either air freight or leftovers.

Two practical notes for Q4: printed packaging has its own lead time on top of production, and the inspection slot is harder to book in October than in July.

## Spring and Easter

A smaller window with a specific character: pastel colours, egg themes, craft kits, small gift items. Because volumes are lower, many buyers attach Easter to the spring order rather than running a separate one.

The constraint is that Easter moves each year. Check the date before committing a production window, not after.

## Summer water play

The biggest category-specific window in this business. Bubble and water toys need to be on shelf by late April in the northern hemisphere, which means shipping in February or March — immediately after the holiday, when capacity is tight and the factory is ramping back up.

Here the answer is to have the goods produced before the holiday and shipped after it, rather than produced after it.

## Back-to-school and the southern summer

July to September is a quieter toy window in the north but a real one for educational items — and simultaneously the run-up to the southern-hemisphere summer. One production run can serve both if the packaging is neutral.

## The habit that fixes all of it

Draw the calendar backwards from the shelf date once, for the whole year, and put the order date for each season on it. Almost every missed season we see is a season that had no order date written down — only an intention.
