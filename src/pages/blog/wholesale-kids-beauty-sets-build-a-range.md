**Short answer:** a wholesale beauty range needs four SKUs in four different roles — entry, core, gift and premium — not four variations of the same idea. Most first orders fail not because the products are bad, but because all four SKUs compete for the same shelf slot.

## The four roles

Here is how our own catalogue maps onto a planogram, using real ex-factory prices at stated MOQ:

| Role | Example | Ex-factory | MOQ |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Entry / impulse** | Unicorn rainbow backpack kit | $5.95 | 108 |
| **Core volume** | Treasure box vanity, 4-colour | $6.42 | 108 |
| **Gift** | Premium complete set with jewellery | $6.88 | 72 |
| **Mid-premium** | Mermaid aluminium case with LED mirror | $9.92 | 120 |
| **Premium / anchor** | 2-layer glitter trolley case | $23.03 | 60 |

Four to five SKUs across that ladder gives a buyer a reason to take a mixed order. Four SKUs all at $6.50 gives them a reason to take one.

## Why the roles matter more than the products

A shelf works by comparison. The premium item is not there to be the best seller — it is there to make the core item look like good value. Remove the anchor and the core price looks expensive.

Conversely, if everything in your range is a $6 kit, you have no entry price for a trial buyer and no gift option for a higher-ticket channel. You have one product in four packagings.

## What distinguishes the roles, concretely

| Role | Case format | Contents | Carton density |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entry | Bag or printed box | Small piece count, 2–4 colours | High — good freight economics |
| Core | Box or small case | 4–9 colours | High |
| Gift | Case with mirror | 9+ colours, accessories | Medium |
| Mid-premium | Aluminium, LED mirror | 12–18 colours, brushes | Low |
| Premium | Trolley, multi-layer | 14–18 colours, full kit | Lowest |

The right-hand column is the part buyers forget: the premium formats are bulky, so their freight per unit is worse even when the ex-factory price is fair. Compare **landed** cost per unit across the range, not ex-factory.

## Depth versus breadth

Two strategies, and they suit different buyers:

**Breadth** — one SKU per role, four roles. Best for a first order, for a marketplace seller testing the category, and for anyone who does not yet know which format sells in their market.

**Depth** — one role, several SKUs. Best once you know the winner. If the $6.42 treasure box outsells everything, the next order should be three more variations of that format, not a trolley case.

The mistake is doing both at once on a first order. Four SKUs across four roles is a test. Four SKUs in one role is a bet, and you have not earned the information to place it yet.

## Accessories inside the kit

Many kids beauty sets bundle non-cosmetic items — bracelets, hair clips, wigs, rings. Two consequences:

1. **The bundle creates a second compliance question.** A bracelet is a children's product, not a cosmetic. Lead and small-parts rules apply to it separately.
2. **It changes the value story.** A kit with a jewellery piece reads as a gift set at a price where the cosmetics alone would read as cheap.

If your market requires documentation, ask which components are covered by which report. A cosmetic safety report does not cover a bracelet.

## What to ask for a range quote

Send the four roles, not four product names:

> "I need one entry, one core, one gift and one premium SKU in kids beauty, target landed cost per role of X, destination Y."

A factory that understands the category will come back with formats matched to the roles. A trading company will send you a catalogue and ask you to pick.

Then compare on landed cost per unit, not ex-factory, and confirm the carton CBM for each SKU before you commit — the premium format will be the surprise.
