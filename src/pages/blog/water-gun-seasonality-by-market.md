**Short answer:** water toys sell into summer, and summer happens twice a year on this planet. A factory in Guangdong can ship a northern-hemisphere season and a southern-hemisphere season from the same production run — if you plan the calendar instead of reacting to it.

## The two windows

**Northern hemisphere** (Europe, North America, East Asia): the selling window is roughly April to August, so goods on shelf by late April. That means a ship date in February or March, and a purchase order in the northern autumn or early winter — before Chinese New Year.

**Southern hemisphere** (Australia, New Zealand, southern South America, southern Africa): the window is roughly November to February, goods on shelf by early November. Ship date August or September, purchase order in the northern spring.

The two calendars are almost exactly half a year apart, which is convenient: it means a mould runs twice a year instead of once.

## Why this matters commercially

A single-season product ties up capital for the rest of the year. A product with two markets has two turns of inventory per year from the same tooling, which changes the economics substantially — and it is why some suppliers can offer better terms on a category that looks seasonal.

The catch is that southern-hemisphere volumes are smaller in most categories, so the second season is a top-up rather than a main run.

## Planning an order across both

- **Place the northern order first**, sized to the main market.
- **Hold a reorder option** for the southern window, agreed at the same unit price.
- **Keep the packaging neutral** — avoid "Summer 2026" printed on the box if you intend to sell the same carton twice.
- **Check the certification per market**, because the two hemispheres are also two regulatory regions.

That last point is the one that surprises buyers: a product tested for the EU is not automatically documented for Australia or Brazil. The season is the easy part; the documentation is the work.

## The Chinese New Year constraint

Both calendars run into the same factory shutdown. The northern order needs to be produced before the holiday; the southern order is usually produced after it, in spring, which is a less congested window for capacity but a tighter one for the ship date.

If you are running both, the northern order is the one to protect. It has less slack.

## What to ask a supplier

- Has this item run in a southern-hemisphere season before?
- Is the packaging seasonal, or neutral?
- Can the same unit price hold for a second smaller run later in the year?
- Which markets is the item documented for today?

A supplier who has run the item for both hemispheres will have opinions about which colours sell where. That is worth more than a price list.

## A note on "seasonal" stock

Water toys do not become worthless in September the way a licensed character item does — the mechanism is generic and the demand returns. What loses value is seasonal packaging. Neutral packaging is the cheapest insurance in this category, and it is usually free.
