**Short answer:** two makeup kits can contain almost identical cosmetics and sell at three times the price difference, because the case is what the customer judges. When you compare suppliers on "number of colours", you are comparing the wrong variable.

## The five formats, in price order

|---|---|---|---|
| Printed box / treasure box | Pink vanity treasure box, 4-colour shadow | **$6.42** | 108 |

The spread is **3.6×** — from $6.42 to $23.03 — on products that all contain eyeshadow and lip gloss. Nothing about the cosmetics explains that gap.

## What actually drives the price

**1. Layers.** A single-layer case opens to one tray. A three-layer case opens like a tackle box into three working surfaces. Each layer adds tooling, assembly time and volume — and it is the single biggest driver of perceived value on a shelf.

**2. The surface treatment.** Plain ABS is cheap. Laser-etched aluminium looks expensive from two metres away. Glitter laminate sits between the two. This is where a $8 case becomes a $23 case with almost no change in contents.

**3. Whether it has a mirror and a light.** An LED mirror converts a case into a "vanity" — different product, different shelf, different price. It also adds a battery, which changes your shipping classification (see our note on lithium below).

**4. Case dimensions and carton efficiency.** A trolley case is bulky. Fewer units per carton means higher freight per unit, which shows up in your landed cost even when the ex-factory price looks reasonable.

**5. MOQ behaviour.** Notice the pattern above: the cheapest format has the *highest* MOQ (108), and the most expensive has one of the lowest (60). Expensive formats are lower-volume by nature; factories set MOQ accordingly.

## Choosing a format, by channel

| Your channel | Sensible format | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Discount retail, high volume | Printed box or soft suitcase | Lowest unit cost, best carton density |
| Mid-market gift | Iron case with mirror | Reads as a gift, still under ten dollars |
| Department store / boutique | Aluminium, 2–3 layer | The case is the display object |
| Online marketplace hero SKU | Trolley case | Photographs well, high perceived value per thumbnail |
| Party favour / low ticket | Bag format | Lowest landed cost, sold in multiples |

## The mistake buyers make

They order the expensive format first. A $23 trolley case at MOQ 60 ties up more capital per SKU than a $6.42 treasure box at MOQ 108, and the expensive format is the harder sell in an unproven market.

The sequence that works: **start with the mid format, prove sell-through, then add the premium format as a second SKU in the same range.** Two SKUs at different price points also give your buyer an anchor — the $23 case makes the $12 case look reasonable.

## A shipping consequence worth flagging early

Any case with an LED mirror contains a battery. That changes air freight eligibility and cost, and it may change the dangerous-goods documentation your forwarder asks for. If you are comparing a mirror case against a non-mirror case, compare the **landed** cost, not the ex-factory price.

## What to ask before you commit

1. Case material and thickness (ABS, iron, aluminium, laminated board)
2. Whether the surface is painted, laminated or laser-etched
3. Number of layers and the working surface area when open
4. Carton quantity and CBM — the trolley format will surprise you here
5. Whether the mirror is LED-lit, and if so, the battery type
